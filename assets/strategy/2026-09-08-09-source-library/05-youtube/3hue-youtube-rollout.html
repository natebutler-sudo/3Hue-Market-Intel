01

The read

The obvious YouTube plan for a compliance advisory firm is to make videos about compliance frameworks and wait for buyers. The keyword data says that plan builds the wrong audience.

YouTube demand in this category is overwhelmingly career demand. People search "ISO 27001 lead implementer training" (8,118/mo), "how to break into AI governance" (4,986/mo) and "how to learn AI governance" (4,972/mo) because they want a job in the field. People with a budget and a deadline search differently, less often, and in far less crowded terms: "how to do an AI governance audit report" gets 4,968 searches a month against a competition score of 5.0 out of 100.

The trap is real and it is visible in a live channel. François B. Arthanas is positioned almost identically to where 3HUE would sit — a credentialed founder selling AI governance advisory. He has 16,700 subscribers, earned almost entirely on CompTIA certification crash courses. His top video has 190,553 views. His video explaining the NIST Cybersecurity Framework has 1,148. His video arguing the actual thesis he sells — "Your Company does NOT exist to be secure" — has 299 views.

The strategy in one line: chase the smallest audience that can actually buy. A 400-view video watched by six heads of risk at regulated companies is worth more to 3HUE than a 40,000-view video watched by people studying for Security+.

There is one genuine tailwind. ISO 42001 searches grew 164% against baseline — 13,560 a month at a competition score of 23.1, with the training, implementation and certification variants all sitting under 15. That is the same shape CMMC had before it created Summit 7, a 158,000-subscriber channel owned by a compliance consulting firm. The window is open now and it is not crowded yet.

04

The channel we're building

Channel: 3HUE — not AiVRIC, not a personal channel. AiVRIC gets its own product channel when it has a product-led motion to feed; today the buyer is buying an advisor. The face on the channel is still Andrew, which is a separate question handled in section 11.

Channel description (first two lines, which is all YouTube shows):

Governed AI and audit-ready security programs, explained by the people who build them. For risk, compliance and technology leaders at regulated and operationally complex companies.

Four franchises. Each one is a repeatable format with a fixed slot, so the team is never staring at a blank page — and each one is lifted from a pattern we watched work on a real channel this week.

The Snapshotweekly · 15–20 min · flagship
Andrew, rotating
One governance problem, taken apart and put back together. Whiteboard or screen-share, no slides. Ends with the artifact — a template, a checklist, a control mapping — linked in the description. This is where the ISO 42001 and AI-governance keywords live.
ControlCase: 19 long uploads in a year → 4.1M views. Depth beats frequency in this category.
The Filingevent-driven · 10–20 min · within 72 hrs
whoever knows the rule
A regulator, standards body or major buyer moves — we explain what it means by Friday. EU AI Act milestones, ISO 42001 amendments, SEC and FFIEC guidance, DORA deadlines, a named enterprise mandating a control in its supplier terms. Speed is the entire product.
Summit 7's four highest-view videos are all this format: 297,798 · 118,968 · 97,337 · 70,623 views.
The Findingsmonthly · 15–25 min · proprietary data
Andrew + Shay
What we actually see across the programs we run. Anonymised and aggregated: the controls that fail most often, the questions that stall a questionnaire, what AiVRIC's checks flag across cloud estates. No client is named without written consent.
Summit 7's "100 CMMC Assessments — What We Now Know" (43,778) and "We Mapped 130 Iranian Cyber Attacks to CMMC" (49,949).
The Roommonthly · 30–50 min · guest
Andrew hosts
A conversation with someone who has been audited, breached, or asked to certify on a deadline. Practitioners over vendors: a bank's cloud governance lead, a PE operating partner, a Type II auditor. The guest's network is the distribution.
Summit 7's Boeing episode hit 108.9 views/hour — the fastest-accelerating video in the entire competitive set.
The Benchbiweekly · 6–10 min · practitioner
Shay · Ariel · Teriah
One mechanism, explained by the person who operates it. Shay on how AI changes incident response, access and monitoring. Ariel on where Power Platform automation helps and where governance has to lead. Teriah on turning a business idea into a controlled AI use case. Short by design — one idea, no preamble.
Added in v2 from the GTM deck's named YouTube cast. This is the franchise that makes the channel survive a month when the founder is unavailable.
On runtime — reconciling with the GTM deck. The GTM deck specifies 6–10 minute explainers. That is right for The Bench, where the job is one mechanism explained cleanly. It is too short for the rest: ControlCase's long-form averages roughly 22 minutes and drew 4.1M views from 19 uploads; Summit 7's averages about 16; the highest-velocity video in the whole competitive set is a 51-minute guest episode. Length follows the franchise, not a single house rule.
Shorts run three a week, cut from footage we already shot. Their only job is reach and their only CTA is the long-form video — never the website. Stuart Barker's channel is the evidence: his Shorts averaged 975 views while his long-form averaged 11,928. Shorts are the top of the funnel, not the funnel.

06

The 12-week rollout

Nothing publishes until the destinations work. Four weeks of build, four weeks of launch, four weeks of proving the cadence holds. Dates are real.

PHASE 0

Build — nothing is public yet

7 Sep – 4 Oct
Week 17–13 Sep
  • Fix the site first — this blocks everything. 3hue.net and www.3hue.net currently serve two different websites; there is no sitemap.xml, no canonical tag, no og:url and zero JSON-LD. Every link in this plan points at a destination that does not resolve consistently. Redirect www → apex, publish a sitemap, add canonicals, add Organization and Person structured data. Strip every "Q2 2026" availability string — it has been stale since 30 June.
  • Stand up the landing page the videos point to: /ai-risk-snapshot. One offer, one form, no navigation — then Calendly, so the booking is qualified and attributed instead of anonymous.
  • Create the YouTube channel, claim the handle, set the description, banner and links. Verify the account so custom thumbnails and links unlock.
  • Confirm the four-person on-camera bench and book the first shoot day into four calendars, not one.
Week 214–20 Sep
  • Script videos 01–04 from the Message Stack. No new claims — every assertion must already exist in the claims library or come with a source.
  • Build the four artifacts that ship with videos 01–04 (RACI template, snapshot scope doc, failed-control list, questionnaire triage sheet).
  • Design the thumbnail system: one template, three variants, brand cyan on dark navy, six words maximum. Test at 210px wide — that is the real size on a phone.
  • Set up tracking: UTM convention, a YouTube traffic segment in analytics, and a "first touch = YouTube" field on the lead record.
Week 321–27 Sep
  • Shoot day one. Videos 01–04 in a single session, plus 12 shorts' worth of B-roll and standalone takes.
  • Edit 01 and 02 to final. Cut six shorts.
  • Write the blog versions of 01–04 for 3hue.net/insights — this doubles as the fix for nine articles all dated 2026-01-20.
  • Warm the LinkedIn audience: Andrew posts twice a week on the same themes, no mention of a channel yet.
Week 428 Sep – 4 Oct
  • Edit 03 and 04. All four videos scheduled, thumbnails A/B ready, chapters and end screens set.
  • Dry run the full linking chain end to end: description link → landing page → form → CRM → follow-up email. Click every link yourself.
  • Brief sales on the slate: which video answers which objection, and the timestamp to send.
  • Line up guest #1 for The Room and confirm their clip rights.
PHASE 1

Launch — four videos, four weeks, full distribution

5 Oct – 1 Nov
Week 55–11 Oct
  • Video 01 publishes Tuesday 09:00 ET. Same-day: Andrew posts the native cut to LinkedIn with the YouTube link in the first comment; company page reposts; blog version goes live with the player embedded.
  • Two shorts Wednesday and Friday, both pointing at 01.
  • Email the house list — link to the blog post, not to YouTube. Keep the click in owned property.
  • Answer every comment within 24 hours in the first month. This is not optional; early engagement is what tells YouTube who the video is for.
Week 612–18 Oct

  • First read on 01: click-through rate, average view duration, traffic sources. Do not touch the title before 72 hours of data.

Week 719–25 Oct

Week 826 Oct – 1 Nov

  • 30-day review. Not "did we get views" — did anyone we want to talk to watch, and did any of them reply. Pull the list of companies from the landing-page fills and score them against the ICP scorer.
  • Decide what gets cut. If a franchise produced nothing in four weeks, it does not get a fifth.

09

How it gets made

The failure mode for a founder-led channel is not bad video — it is week seven, when the founder has a client emergency and nothing ships. Everything below is designed so the channel survives a bad month.

Batch, don't dripone shoot day per month
Four long-form videos and twelve shorts' worth of material in a single session. One camera, one lav, one screen capture, one room. A month of publishing bought with one day of Andrew's calendar.
Summit 7 published 5 long videos in a year and drew 3.3M views. Cadence discipline matters more than cadence speed.
The reserved slotone editing day held weekly
The Filing cannot be batched — it exists to be fast. Hold one day a week empty. If nothing moves that week, that day goes to thumbnail refreshes on old videos, which is the highest-return work available after week eight.
Summit 7's top four videos are all same-week reactions to regulatory events.
Two people, clear splitsubject-matter + production
Andrew supplies the argument and the on-camera delivery. Marketing owns scripting from the Message Stack, the artifact that ships with each video, thumbnails, publishing, distribution and comments. Andrew never touches the publishing chain.
Per the delegation: decisions do not wait on him. Only the recording does.
Kit — deliberately smallunder $1,500 all in
A decent camera or a recent phone on a tripod, a lavalier mic, one soft key light, and a quiet room with a non-distracting background. Audio is the only thing worth spending on. Viewers forgive soft focus; they leave on bad sound.
Every high-performing channel in the competitive set is a talking head or a screen share. None of them look expensive.
Publish specnon-negotiable checklist
Chapters on every video. Custom thumbnail, six words max, legible at 210px. First two description lines carry the CTA. End screen in the last 20 seconds. Pinned comment with the artifact. Closed captions reviewed, never auto-only — the terminology gets mangled and the transcript is what the blog post is built from.
Auto-captions consistently mistranscribe "ISO 42001", "SOC 2 Type II" and "ISMS".

12

What we're not doing

Each of these is a plausible idea we are declining on purpose, so nobody re-proposes it in week six.

Certification training contentThe largest demand in this category — and it builds an audience of job seekers. This is the single documented failure mode in the competitive set.
A vCISO franchise"vCISO", "virtual CISO" and "vCISO services" all return under 750 searches a month. There is no YouTube audience to serve, which corroborates the near-zero view counts on existing vCISO videos.
Chasing "what is AI governance"13,131 searches a month at competition 44. Definitional traffic that watches thirty seconds and never books anything.
Shorts-firstCheaper to make and twelve times less effective per video on the one channel where we can compare both formats directly.
Daily postingTwo channels in the set published 118 and 101 long videos in a year, for roughly 170 and 810 views each. Frequency is not the variable.
Naming clients on cameraNot without written consent. "A large North American bank" remains the correct phrasing until a release is signed — which is worth pursuing, because the Findings franchise gets materially stronger with a name attached.
Paid promotion before month fourPaying to distribute content we have not proven organically buys reach for a message that may still be wrong. Retargeting warm viewers is the exception, and starts in week nine.
A second channel for AiVRICNot this quarter. One channel with a real cadence beats two with a stalled one.
