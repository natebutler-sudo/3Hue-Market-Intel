How to read this

Reading key

vidIQ returns several different measures that are easy to conflate. They are not interchangeable.

Volume
Estimated monthly YouTube searches. Anything reported as <750 is below vidIQ's measurement floor — treat as zero.
Competition
0–100. How many videos already target the term. Under 15 is genuinely open; over 30 is crowded.
Overall
vidIQ's blended opportunity score. Useful for ranking within a cluster, not across clusters.
Growth %
vidIQ's own current-vs-baseline measure. Its baseline window is not disclosed and moves between pulls.
Week Δ
Our own 2 Sep → 9 Sep comparison of the volume figure. Independent of vidIQ's growth %.
VPH / Breakout
Views per hour since publish, and vidIQ's over-performance score vs. the channel's own norm. Breakout > 30 is a genuine outlier.

One caveat that applies everywhere

Several unrelated terms return identical growth figures (−45.8% appears three times; +84.5% appears four times across separate pulls). That strongly suggests vidIQ bands its growth metric rather than reporting a continuous value. Trust the direction and the rank order; do not quote the magnitude as precise.

Finding 01

The crossover: the market is trading a concept for a standard

Between the 2 September and 9 September pulls, the abstract AI-governance vocabulary fell and the concrete standard vocabulary rose. One week is not a trend, but the direction is consistent across five of six terms and it matches what the video-level data shows on Dejan Kosutic's channel.

What this licenses

Stop titling videos "AI governance." Start titling them "ISO 42001." The concept term is 2.7× bigger but falling and sits at competition 42.1; the standard term is rising, sits at 23.1, and its best sub-term (iso 42001 training, competition 8.5) carries the highest opportunity score of any term in the entire research set — 71.0.

This does not change 3HUE's positioning. The site can keep saying "enterprise AI needs a governing layer." It changes what goes in the title bar, which is a different decision made by a different audience.

The pattern to exploit

Every SOC 2 term that includes an explanatory word — explained, checklist, audit, report — sits at single-digit competition. Every term that includes a transactional word — certification, cost, vendors, software — is below the measurement floor. People come to YouTube to understand SOC 2, and go to Google to buy it. Build the channel for the first behaviour and let the site handle the second.

Three things this overturns

1 · Product content is not a tax on the channel — it is the channel. His #1, #2, #3, #4 and #5 videos are all product walkthroughs, together carrying 2.9M views. This is a direct upgrade for "The Stack" (AiVRIC): it should be a front-line series, not a supporting one. The framing that works is "How does it work? [product overview]" — a demonstration, not a pitch.

2 · The interview format underperforms on the largest channel in the niche. His two long consultant interviews (60 and 43 minutes) sit near the bottom of his top-50 at 31K and 59K, against a channel median far higher. And the one explicitly CEO-framed video — "What CEOs need to know about ISO 27001?" — is his weakest at 22,885. That is the third independent data point against making CEO's Corner the flagship.

3 · ISO 42001 works right now. He entered the topic in late 2025 and every one of six videos cleared 58,000 views, with the flagship carrying a 142.5 breakout score. The window is open and 3HUE has a real claim to it.

The caveat that stops this being over-read

The huge explainer numbers are consumer-grade security topics — phishing, passwords, VPNs. They almost certainly convert nobody into a SOC 2 engagement. Take the format lesson, not the topic lesson: 7–10 minutes, plain English, one question per video, no slide deck. Then point that format at the buyer questions in §3 rather than at password hygiene.

The webinar evidence is the durable finding. Seven recorded webinars, none above 1,700 views, several under 700 — on a channel that averages 36,822. Do not put a webinar on the channel.

Volume in this category is career intent, not buying intent

Dejan Kosutic can serve that demand profitably because he sells courses. 3HUE does not. Chasing the biggest numbers in this niche builds the wrong audience.

The pattern

Specificity + stakes + a number beats category phrasing every time. The conventional enterprise-marketing construction — "Best Practices for Enterprise Organizations" — is the lowest score in the set despite being the phrasing most B2B teams default to.

Worth noting where score and search demand disagree: the 78-scoring ISO 42001 title uses iso 42001 explained, which is below the measurement floor, while iso 42001 training at 6,946/mo and competition 8.5 is unclaimed. A title can be well-built and still point at nothing.

Limits

What this data does not tell you

  • vidIQ credits are exhausted as of 9 Sep. Two planned pulls did not complete: the EU AI Act / DORA / NIS2 regulatory-reaction cluster, and buyer-intent terms around security questionnaires and third-party risk. Both matter for the regulatory-reaction series — top them up before committing to that lane.
  • Search volume is not buying intent. The Francois B. Arthanas case in §12 is the proof. Nothing in this data distinguishes a CISO from a job-seeker typing the same words, and roughly 21,600 monthly searches in the ISO 27001 cluster alone are visibly career-driven.
  • Growth percentages are banded, not continuous. Identical figures recur across unrelated terms. Direction and rank order are reliable; magnitude is not.
  • One week is not a trend. The crossover in §1 is directionally consistent across five of six terms and matches the video-level evidence, but it needs a third and fourth data point before anyone bets a year of production on it. Re-pull the same six terms weekly.
  • No competitor conversion data exists. View counts say nothing about whether ControlCase or Summit 7 closes business from YouTube. The customer-story films and sales-objection explainers imply they believe it does, but that is inference.
  • 3HUE's own numbers are still missing — Search Console, GA, CRM won/lost, LinkedIn page analytics. Everything here is inferred from competitors. That gap is unchanged from the Maturity Gap research.
