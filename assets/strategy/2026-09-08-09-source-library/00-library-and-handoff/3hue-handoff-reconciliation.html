02

Six things we didn't know

These are facts present in the handed-over documents and absent from every public source I researched. Each one changes something.

01

3HUE is a Veteran-Owned Small Business

The 2024 deck states it plainly on the About Our Company slide, alongside "Founded in 2015" and "2020 Go-To-Market". It appears on no current page of 3hue.net, in no third-party listing I found, and in none of the eight documents we have built. For the regulated and federal-adjacent buyers in the ICP — and for supplier-diversity programs inside large enterprises — this is a qualification, not a nice-to-have.
ActionConfirm VOSB vs SDVOSB and current SBA registration before it goes anywhere public. Then it belongs in the site footer, the LinkedIn page, and the claims library.
02

There is a four-person on-camera bench

The GTM deck names the YouTube cast: Andrew on board-level AI risk, Shay on how AI changes incident response and monitoring, Ariel on Power Platform automation and where governance must lead, Teriah on turning business ideas into controlled AI use cases. This answers the one open question left in The Rollout.
ActionAlready applied. The Rollout v2 adds a fifth franchise, The Bench, and closes the founder-dependency risk.
03

3HUE has published real prices before

The 2024 site carried "Starting as low as $15 per endpoint" on the MDR page, with a $15 EDR and $45 XDR CTA pair, and named SentinelOne as the agent. The current site publishes nothing. The 2024 website was commercially more transparent than the 2026 one.
ActionRemoves the "we don't publish pricing" objection to a published pricing architecture. There is precedent, and it was 3HUE's own.
04

The old quote form is a ready-made ROI model

The three-step "Get a Quote" flow collected user endpoints, server endpoints, AWS and Azure accounts, mobile devices, technology assets, vendors with access to sensitive data, physical locations, users, employees, admin users — plus yes/no on PCI-DSS, HITRUST, SOC 2, CMMC and HIPAA. That is a complete scoping input set, and it is 3HUE's own historical logic rather than something invented.
ActionUse it as the input model for the ROI calculator still on the build list, instead of designing one from scratch.
05

Named partners nobody is using

The 2024 material names SentinelOne as the EDR/XDR technology, Roar Technical Inspectors as a partner, "White Dog" as the whitepaper producer, and HubSpot's free tier as the email platform. None appear in current materials, and the LinkedIn company page had 211 followers at the time of the screenshot.
ActionConfirm which relationships are live. Partner logos and a co-marketing motion are among the cheapest credibility available.
06

Three public surfaces, two founding years

The site and the 2024 deck say founded 2015. BBB lists incorporation on 27 July 2015. The LinkedIn company About text, screenshotted inside the 2024 deck, says "established in 2017 and debuted its one-of-a-kind Virtual CIO managed services in 2020."
ActionResolve before any "X years in business" claim ships. A buyer who checks two sources and finds two answers stops checking.
03

Where it all converges

This is worth stating plainly, because it is unusual. The GTM deck was written from the 2024 material and a website review. Our eight documents were written from public research, social listening and keyword data. Neither saw the other. They arrive at the same place on seven substantive calls.

Snapshot → Build → Operate as the offer architecture= The Capability Map
An ICP defined by buying pain, urgency and readiness rather than demographics= The Forcing Function
Buying triggers: board pressure, tool adoption, governance gap, customer assurance, audit pressure, shadow AI= the trigger work
Channel jobs: Website converts · LinkedIn distributes · YouTube teaches · Email nurtures · Search gets you found= The Rollout linking map
"Measure pipeline quality, not vanity traffic" — with YouTube watch time explicitly demoted to a secondary health metric= The Rollout 90-day KPI
A 90-day shape: fix the site and messaging, then launch LinkedIn and the first YouTube batch, then run the campaign and report pipeline= the 12-week rollout
Case studies packaged five ways: web page, one-page PDF, LinkedIn post, short video, sales proof slide= The Ship List
What this is worth. Independent convergence is the strongest evidence available that the direction is right. It also means the marketing leader and this workstream are not competing plans — the GTM deck is the strategy at leadership altitude, and the eight documents are the execution layer underneath it. Say that out loud when you hand this back.
04

Four conflicts, resolved

Where the documents disagree, here is the call and the evidence behind it. All four are already applied.

A · How long should the videos be?
GTM deck"6–10 minute explainers."
The dataControlCase's long-form averages about 22 minutes — 19 uploads produced 4.1M views. Summit 7's averages about 16. The highest-velocity video in the entire competitive set is a 51-minute guest episode at 108.9 views per hour.

Resolved: length follows the franchise, not a house rule. 6–10 minutes is right for The Bench, where the job is one mechanism explained cleanly. 15–25 for The Snapshot and The Findings, 30–50 for The Room.

B · Are cybersecurity professionals a target audience?
2024 deckLists "Cybersecurity Professionals" as a target audience, with broad category blog titles to match.
The dataPractitioners searching YouTube are overwhelmingly career-intent, and every broad category term is contracting: cyber security −23.0%, cybersecurity −18.9%, information security −36.3%, ai ethics −45.8%.

Resolved: the GTM deck already corrects this by moving the ICP off demographics. Practitioners stay as an influence audience — people who recommend 3HUE — not a demand-generation target.

C · What still needs leadership approval?
GTM deckSlide 16 requests sign-off on four items: positioning and ICP, Snapshot-first CTA, the 90-day rollout, and website fixes.
RealityAndrew has explicitly delegated marketing direction. Three of the four are decided and documented.

Resolved: only the website fix still needs something from leadership — and what it needs is an owner and developer hours, not a marketing decision.

D · Is link-building the SEO priority?
2024 deckRecommends HARO/Connectively, prospecting, email outreach and the skyscraper technique.
The dataThe site already had 155 backlinks from 33 referring domains in 2024 — and zero organic traffic. Links were never the constraint.

Resolved: fix indexation first — one hostname, a sitemap, canonicals, structured data. Link-building is a later-order problem and wasted effort until a crawler can tell which site is the site.

05

What carries forward

The 2024 deck is two and a half years old and most of it has been superseded. These five pieces have not been, and re-deriving them would be waste.

The tone & voice frameworkEight attributes, and "Confidence without Arrogance" is the best line in either document. It survives intact into the brand standard.
The consumer journey mapAwareness → Consideration → Acquisition → Service → Loyalty, with real touchpoints named at each stage. Still the right spine for the nurture program.
The "Company & Culture" navigationOur Story · Meet the Team · Careers · Contact, alongside Life at 3HUE · Partners · Awards & Certifications. The current site has no equivalent, and a founder-led firm needs one.
Non-LinkedIn social carries culture, not leadsCorrect in 2024 and correct now. It saves the team from trying to make five platforms do demand generation.
The buyer-persona disciplineKeep the method — how they research, how they want to be reached, what they are measured on. Drop the demographic detail; "45–54, mostly male" is not a targeting instruction.
The GTM deck's buying committeeRisk · Executive · Finance · Tech · Operations, each needing the message translated. This is better than anything in our documents and gets absorbed into the message stack.

07

What to say back

A suggested reply to the marketing leader, in the order that respects the work:

1. The GTM deck holds up. Independent research arrived at the same offer architecture, the same trigger list, the same channel roles and the same "pipeline over vanity metrics" KPI stance. That is not a coincidence worth glossing over — it means the strategy is right and we can stop debating direction.

2. Two things in the handover we did not have and needed. The named on-camera bench unblocked the YouTube plan's single biggest risk. And the veteran-owned status may be the most under-used asset in the company — nobody outside these documents knows about it.

3. One thing needs an owner, not another document. The website was diagnosed as non-ranking in February 2024. The GTM deck re-diagnosed it and named the highest-priority fix. That fix is still not done, the site now serves two different companies at two spellings of the domain, and the homepage is advertising a quarter that ended in June. Nothing else in any plan compounds until this is fixed.

4. The ask. A name and a date against the website work. Everything else can proceed in parallel, and will.
