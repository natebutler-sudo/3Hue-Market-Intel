## The question (01:19:57–01:20:17)

Nate asks whether "hue" is a play on something — he has seen the word used elsewhere to mean different shades — and whether there is "a play there."

## 1. Where it came from: three people, three hues (01:20:17–01:23:11)

- Andrew was head of IT and security at Life Extension. A colleague — his senior manager of business intelligence and data analytics — was impressed by him, took him to lunch, and pitched starting a company: a friend of his was CTO at a merchant-capital firm and could get them in if Andrew had a business vehicle.
- Andrew's condition: go to Las Vegas first. Five days, no business talk. "I can't rock with people who aren't real people… I curse, I'm a real person, I'm not filtering myself… that only translates with some, and I will never change that." The point was to find out whether they got along as people.
- Day five, at a cafe *[name garbled; "grandma's cafe"]*, he told them he was convinced. His colleague asked what to call the company. The transcript is badly garbled here, but the thread is: the three of them sitting there, the three… *[reconstructed: three hues]*. "Was it Googled?" — they checked the name.
- When Nate asks directly (01:24:37) whether the name was freestyled or "based on the three people that came up with the idea," Andrew: **"[We] were all of different hues."** And at 01:27:43: **"The name is about inclusion. It's an inclusive name, because it considers three parts…"** *[next words garbled; sounds like "of all ethnicity"]*.

## 2. How it got its shape (01:23:11–01:24:30)

- "I move fast." Back in Florida he needed a final name for the articles of incorporation and would not sit on it.
- The first rendering "looked like a pizza parlor" / like somebody's last name. He set it in all caps and closed the space: **3 HUE → 3HUE.** ("It had three, space, HUE, all caps, then I brought it together.")
- "3HUE Consulting" sounded off — "we don't consult for paint" — so: what do we consult for? Executives. **3HUE Executive Consulting.**
- Implication for the Brand Sheet: the all-caps, no-space convention is the founder's, from the incorporation filing onward.

## 3. The marketing reading he tried and dropped (01:24:55–01:25:11)

- Afterwards he tried to make the name market: **"the three hues of security."** "I tried to do it over and… we went around the whole world with it." He dropped it.
- This matters because the Brand Sheet v1 reading (three = three practices) is the same move. It has already been tried by the person who owns the name.

## 4. The meaning he holds now: a standard, not a noun (01:25:11–01:27:38)

- "One day the true meaning came." Blake kept pressing him on what the three meant; Andrew's first reaction was that he did not care. Blake: "Well, what does it mean to *you*?"
- Andrew: **"You do things at the highest possible level of execution. That's what 3HUE is."** "It doesn't sound like an acronym, right? It describes doing things at the highest possible [level]."
- He uses the name **as an adjective** — "a magic adjective," "I'm very adjective with it":
  - "When you get a 3HUE on it… that's like special forces. It's like a tiger team, right? Don't use the word tiger team — get a 3HUE on that."
  - "This is the most critical thing we have. We need a 3HUE on that."
- The competitive claim behind it: "It may look the same on a line card, but it is not the same when you look behind what actually gets brought and delivered." "When you're looking for excellence and pursuing excellence, that's when you get a 3HUE on it."
- He wants it to be a credential, including for consultants: "even consultants — this is the holy grail, to get a 3HUE. You made it. You're better than [the rest]. You carry the brand."
- Why he guards the brand: "What we do has a master [standard], but so does what we say. If I just give you a [deliverable], it can't look like a document another [person] created. That is a nightmare for me. No — it is the highest possible level of execution. We think about everything, we obsess about it. Our backgrounds look great, everything looks great. That's 3HUE. That's the standard of excellence you've come to know, love and expect."

## 5. The three (01:27:38–01:29:20)

- "The concept applies… you can apply this across different aspects." He is open to layered readings as long as the name itself is not shrunk to one.
- Three resonates with him without planning: "every business that I have started has a connotation of the number three, and I didn't plan it that way." He owns **Trilogy Multimedia Enterprises**. "All great things come in threes." "The Holy Trinity." A documentary on Tesla and 3-6-9.
- "Some things you can't explain. It just came to you, you knew it was real."
- Coda (01:33:30): "3HUE was the first step… what comes after that is where we go."
