# 3HUE competitive landscape and public proof

Research date: September 9, 2026. Discovery only; no product implementation. This report separates publisher statements, external corroboration, and analyst inference. Competitor inclusion means observable service overlap, not evidence of a head-to-head deal. Sources were examined through public web search and extracted page content; no paid databases, private customer records, or vendor demos were used.

## Executive assessment

3HUE has a traceable public business footprint beyond its own website: a Florida corporate-index entry, a LinkedIn company profile, a HubSpot Solutions Partner profile, and an Intrusion partner-directory entry. These support identity and channel presence, not revenue, delivery quality, or current commercial terms. Its named customer stories are useful first-party evidence, but independent customer-side confirmation was not found in the targeted searches conducted.

The most relevant comparison set spans three buying alternatives: practitioner-led security/GRC advisory, broader AI risk consultancies, and software used by internal teams. Combining these into one undifferentiated competitor ranking would be misleading. CBIZ Pivot Point Security and Fractional CISO are useful service-model comparisons; Coalfire and Protiviti overlap with AI governance/readiness; Schellman is an adjacent assurance provider; Vanta and Drata are software alternatives and possible ecosystem participants. These are provisional candidates until 3HUE supplies win/loss history.

Analyst inference: 3HUE's strongest defensible differentiation hypothesis is a compact, senior-led journey from a bounded AI diagnostic to implemented governance and ongoing operations. “AI governance,” “continuous compliance,” and “business-aligned security” are shared category language, not unique claims. The eventual intelligence product should expose changes to offers, delivery models, assurance evidence, and buyer problems rather than simply collect generic AI headlines.

## Practical intelligence implications

These are recommendations for later discovery, not an approved build specification:

1. Keep a **service competitor** list separate from **software alternatives**, **assurance providers**, and **partners**. A vendor can occupy multiple categories; preserve that ambiguity.
2. Track changes to offer scope, promised time to outcome, delivery personnel, evidence requirements, availability stage, and customer proof. These are more useful to campaign and sales enablement than unqualified mention counts.
3. Attach evidence labels to every proof point: vendor claim, named first-party case, external directory listing, customer corroboration, independent attestation, or analyst inference.
4. Avoid automatically producing attack-style battlecards. Use verified differences and questions buyers should ask; surface missing data openly.
5. Treat internal staffing or “do nothing yet” as research hypotheses for alternative buying behavior. Public competitor pages alone do not show their prevalence in 3HUE opportunities.
6. Prioritize monitoring around the actual buyer problem: stalled AI rollout, customer-assurance friction, governance ownership, compliance evidence, and security execution. Validate these priorities against 3HUE's eventual ICP and deal history.

## Method and limitations

Queries combined the exact company name/domain with clients, partners and AiVRIC, and used official competitor domains for capability discovery. Unrelated “3Hue” search hits, data-broker contact details, Reddit anecdotes, unsourced market estimates and directory-generated competitor lists were excluded from conclusions. The report does not reproduce source documents or scrape gated material. Extracted pages and search indexes can lag live content; access date is not publication date. Search-engine relative ages were not converted into exact dates. Links and attributed summaries make findings reviewable, but absence from this finite search is not proof of absence.
